Q&A Study Notes:
Typography in Motion Graphics

What is the difference between text and type?
  • Text: Refers to the linguistic content of the collection of words.
  • Type: Refers to the graphical "container". It is the optional shape and style of letterforms that gives a word or phrase a personality regardless of its meanings.
What are the consequences of failing to integrate typography into a composition?
Beginners often treat type as an afterthought, simply pasting it onto a canvas after other visual elements are in place. This gives the impression that the type is a secondary consideration. Ultimately, typography that is not properly integrated will flatten out the overall composition. Instead, type should be treated like any other visual element, applying consistent styling to space, value, contrast, or color.
What are the basic terms and principles in typographic design?
  • Serif vs. Sans Serif: Serif fonts are traditional or classical; their horizontal strokes help to reinforce and maintain a sense of separate lines. Sans serif fonts are considered contemporary and modern, commonly used for headlines and display signage, but difficult to read in large amounts.
  • Baseline: An invisible line upon which the bottoms of letters sit.
  • Kerning: The space between two individual letterforms.
  • Tracking: The space between all of the letters in a word.
  • Leading: The space between lines of typography.
  • Visual Hierarchy: This can be created with size, weight, color, texture, typeface choices and combinations, orientation, and space.
Why is kinetic typography important in motion graphics?
Kinetic typography adds the dimension of time and movement. It is important for two primary reasons:
  • Visual Interest: Movement of type is more visually-involving and promotes a cinematic experience rather than the static type of a book.
  • Embedding Emotion: Different kinetic techniques on text can evoke varied sentiments to the viewer. Fast, strong, and contrasting animation can suggest confidence, power, or solidity, while gentler, slower animation can suggest calmness or fragility.
What are some categories and patterns of animated typography?
  • Motion Typography: Letters and words may move away from one another on a 2D plane, in three-dimensional space, or scroll across the flat screen.
  • Fluid Typography: Letterforms change and evolve without necessarily changing location.
  • Morphing: Lettering can morph into non-typographical elements, focusing on structural changes to the elements themselves.
  • Inventive Metaphors: Individual characters can take on real-world characteristics (for instance, the serifs on certain fonts can become feet that walk).
  • Arc / Path Motion: Movement occurs along arcs or abstract lines as text moves along linear or curved paths.
Where can we find animated typography in game design?
In game design, animated typography is commonly explored in "Press Start" screens, cut scenes, intros, finales, in-game UI, and game promo trailers.

Answers to the End-of-Lesson Questions

1. What is the difference between text and type?
Text represents the linguistic content of the collection of words. Type is the graphical "container", representing the shape and style of letterforms that gives a word or phrase its personality regardless of its actual meaning.
2. What are the consequences of failing to integrate typography as a component in a composition?
Treating type as an afterthought makes it appear as a secondary consideration rather than an integrated visual element. Consequently, typography that is not integrated properly will flatten out the overall composition.
3. Why kinetic typography is important for motion graphics?
Kinetic typography is important because it adds visual interest by promoting a cinematic, visually-involving experience. Additionally, it embeds emotion, as different kinetic techniques (like fast/strong versus slow/gentle animations) can evoke specific sentiments such as confidence, power, calmness, or fragility.
4. What is an example of animated typography?
Examples include fluid typography where letterforms change and evolve without changing location, morphing where letters structurally change into non-typographical elements, and inventive metaphors where individual font characters take on real-world characteristics, such as serifs becoming walking feet.
5. Where can we find kinetic typography in game design?
We can find kinetic typography in game design within "Press Start" screens, cut scenes, intros, finales, in-game sequences, and game promo trailers.
