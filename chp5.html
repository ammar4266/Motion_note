Q&A Study Notes: Motion Choreography

Why do we choreograph motion in motion graphics?
Motion can be choreographed to impart knowledge, communicate information, convey emotions, and express pure aesthetic beauty. The method chosen to move an element across the screen can heavily enhance its meaning. For example, a line of text that animates slowly while fading up from black might imbue the scene with a sense of mystery. Conversely, if the same text flips over and whizzes across the screen, it may express a sense of playfulness or urgency. Ultimately, the motion itself can be the message.
What are the main examples of Spatial Transformations?
Spatial transformations include:
  • Positioning: The translation of the x, y, or z axis.
  • Orientation: Rotation that involves changing the angle the object faces around a central anchor point (point of origin).
  • Size: The relative scale of an object inside the frame.
These properties can be combined, allowing you to animate all properties simultaneously.
How do direction and motion paths work in choreography?
Motion paths specify the course of travel that elements take over a given time interval.
  • Linear: Travel in a straight line in predictable, linear directions (e.g., pendulums, wind-up toys).
  • Nonlinear: Travel on a curve in unpredictable, erratic ways (e.g., water, or tree branches affected by the wind).
  • Arcs: With the exception of mechanical devices, most actions travel in arcs, which are slightly circular paths (e.g., gravity or human body joints).
How does frame mobility (camera movement) play a role in motion graphics?
Frame mobility is achieved through actual or simulated camera movement. It breathes life into scenes and helps achieve various dynamic compositional framings. Common camera techniques include the pan, whip pan, tilt, dolly, zoom, boom/jib, and crane shots.
Is composition important in motion graphics?
Yes, in conjunction with camera movement, establishing a correct composition within the frame helps to build the plot or the major idea being conveyed.
What are the 12 Principles of Animation applied in motion graphics?
The principles include Squash and Stretch, Anticipation, Arcs, Ease In & Ease Out, Appeal, Timing, Solid Drawing, Exaggeration, Pose to Pose, Staging, Secondary Action, and Follow Through and Overlap.
